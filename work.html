Operating evidence

Selected Work

Across small-business operations, enterprise AI strategy, university governance, and live broadcasting, the recurring pattern is the same: find leverage hidden by inherited assumptions, intervene across functional boundaries, and leave durable capability behind. These cases show the full sequence—perception, execution, and institutionalization—through deployed systems, decision methods, governance structures, automation, training, and repeatable operating processes. The point is not a collection of unrelated wins, but a repeatable way of turning judgment into systems an organization can keep using.

01 / Operating modernization

Fairfield Industrial Sales

Operating modernization under real constraints

A collection of software, data, forms, infrastructure, and task-management failures revealed one larger operating problem: critical knowledge and processes were trapped in people and brittle tools rather than represented in durable systems.

Situation
A small industrial business depended on aging software, tacit knowledge, ad hoc task management, and fragile operational infrastructure. Sage dependence, missing data, email and infrastructure issues, backup risk, and opaque work queues created overlapping continuity risks.
Leverage found
The individual failures were not separate technical tickets. They were evidence that the operating model itself needed to become more explicit, recoverable, and controllable.
Intervention
Nichole reconstructed important workflows, recovered operations-essential data after an outside contractor had failed to restore it, replaced Sage-dependent forms, designed and deployed a local-first Fairfield Ops Hub, introduced recovery controls, modernized supporting infrastructure, and trained the primary user through adoption.
Organizational effect
Core forms became independent of Sage; recurring Sage 50 licensing was eliminated, saving approximately $128.67 per month; the primary operator's work moved from an ad hoc "salad of tasks" toward a visible workflow; and continuity became less dependent on a single brittle failure path.
Capability left behind
A maintainable operational system, reusable forms and workflow components, explicit recovery mechanisms, and a more legible operating model remained behind—not another layer of dependence on the person who built it.

Evidence

Deployed and maintained operating system; working Sage-independent forms; recovered operations-essential data; implemented continuity controls; verified recurring-cost removal; active-user training and adoption support.

02 / AI strategy

ServiceNow AI Value Investigation

Turning an ambiguous AI thesis into a falsifiable operating program

Independent, outside-in strategy work converting a broad question about enterprise AI value realization into a concrete decision framework, diagnostic, and bounded validation proposal.

Situation
Enterprise AI discussions often move from technical capability directly to presumed business value without tracing where value is lost across workflow integration, governance, human behavior, measurement, and organizational learning.
Leverage found
The opportunity was not another AI feature proposal. It was a method for locating value-realization failure and deciding whether a proposed opportunity deserved further investment at all.
Intervention
Nichole investigated enterprise AI adoption and value failure, produced a six-page executive memo and one-page diagnostic, separated assumptions from validation requirements, designed a bounded validation proposal, and defined an explicit stop condition rather than presuming the thesis should continue indefinitely.
Organizational effect
A broad strategic hypothesis became a concrete decision framework and testable operating proposal. This is strategic proof work; it was not commissioned or adopted by ServiceNow and is not presented as a deployed transformation.
Capability left behind
A reusable diagnostic for tracing value loss across capability, workflow, governance, behavior, measurement, and learning, plus a bounded structure for deciding whether further investment is warranted.

Evidence

The complete public proof package includes the strategy brief, validation logic, stop conditions, one-page diagnostic instrument, and supporting public evidence.

03 / Institutional leadership

UCR Graduate Student Association

Institutional leadership across power, governance, and crisis

  • 4,000+graduate-student constituents
  • ~$628Kportfolio
  • 20budgeted officer roles
Situation
A large representative institution operated across university administration, budgets, policy, health access, immigration, public safety, graduate-student representation, labor actions, and COVID-era crisis conditions.
Leverage found
Issues framed as isolated advocacy requests often reflected missing accountability, decision rights, or recurring governance mechanisms. The larger opportunity was to make them governable.
Intervention
As president, Nichole coordinated crisis work involving health access, immigration threats, and clinical-placement continuity; developed and passed a public-safety oversight restructuring proposal; and challenged the prevailing athletics austerity model with quantitative analysis and an alternative used during the decision process.
Organizational effect
Urgent problems were moved into formal decision channels; public-safety accountability was restructured; and the athletics intervention helped broaden the decision set during a review that preserved Division I athletics.
Capability left behind
Governance structures, committees, policies, reporting mechanisms, and recurring decision processes through which the institution could continue acting after the immediate intervention.

Evidence

Institutional scale and portfolio responsibility; approved governance proposal; crisis operating record; budget and policy responsibilities; documented athletics-analysis contribution. Confidential institutional working files are not republished here.

04 / Operating systems

WVUR

Designing operations that survive turnover

  • ~50DJs
  • Livebroadcast operations
  • Recurringtraining and turnover
Situation
A student radio operation had to coordinate roughly 50 DJs, a management team, live broadcasting, recurring and one-off schedules, training requirements, and predictable organizational turnover.
Leverage found
Scheduling, training, and handoffs did not have to remain recurring coordination problems. They could be represented as repeatable operating structures.
Intervention
Nichole managed DJs and managers, created recurring and one-off schedules, trained DJs in live broadcast and studio production, standardized operating practices, supported broadcast automation, and developed clearer role and handoff structures.
Organizational effect
Operations became more repeatable, recurring coordination burden fell, and scheduling and training depended less on tacit knowledge held by a few people.
Capability left behind
Scheduling, automation, training, and role structures designed to keep supporting the station through routine turnover rather than requiring the original designer to remain in the loop.

Evidence

Operating schedules, broadcast automation, repeatable training, standardized practices, and role/handoff structures.
