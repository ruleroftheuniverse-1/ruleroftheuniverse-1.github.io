Independent outside-in strategy · 14 August 2026

Test the feedback loop after AI acts.

Use a bounded paid test in IT service workflows to learn whether workflow outcomes can improve customers’ next AI-management decision—and whether ServiceNow should invest in making that loop easier.

Strategy briefOne-page diagnosticEvidence

Why this exists

ServiceNow is increasingly turning AI from advice into action through workflows. The question I test here is what happens after action: do workflow outcomes become reusable knowledge that improves the next AI-management decision? I propose a bounded paid market test using an AI Value Investigation Case—a structured record of the decision, competing explanations, evidence, next test, and retained learning. ServiceNow is unusually positioned because the AI asset, workflow evidence, accountable people, changes, and outcomes can often be connected in one operating environment. The uncertainty is deliberate: existing ServiceNow capabilities may already close this loop, or customers may not value the remaining gap enough to pay.

Strategy brief

Recommended next decision

Run a bounded paid market test—not a product or service launch—through Impact/FDE and existing platform capabilities. Precommit a small delivery-resource ceiling, use only paying design partners as demand evidence, and add no dedicated product engineering. Stop when the ceiling is reached and decide from observed evidence whether the durable answer is existing configuration, better visibility or packaging, a repeatable accelerator, automation, a product—or nothing new.

Your AI strategy already gets from context to action

Your public story is unusually consistent: AI, data, and workflows belong on one platform; AI should not just think, it should act. AI Control Tower connects strategy, governance, management, and performance, while Process Mining shows how workflows and AI agents execute and can be improved. The strategic question here begins after that action occurs.

The question is not whether your platform can “decide with context.” It is whether the results of AI-enabled work become reusable knowledge good enough to improve the next management decision about that AI: preserve it, scale it, change it, replace it, or stop.

AI acts → outcomes are observed → explanations are tested → knowledge is retained → the next AI-management decision improves.

You already have pieces of this loop. Problem Management investigates causes and records lessons; Knowledge Management retains reusable resolutions; Continual Improvement Management turns improvement opportunities into governed work; Process Mining analyzes execution; AI Control Tower measures value and adoption; and the current “Identify ways to improve service” agentic workflow analyzes feedback, metrics, and trends to recommend service improvements. The market test asks whether those pieces already close the loop for AI management, whether the gap is mainly visibility or packaging, or whether customers still face a repeatable decision problem worth paying to investigate.

Why start in IT service workflows

IT service is a strong first wedge because the decisions are financially legible, the evidence is relatively rich, changes can often be reversed, and you already have a mature human-service learning loop to benchmark. The point is not that IT is proven best; it is that it lets you learn with less invention.

What one AI Value Investigation does

The market test is your experiment; each customer investigation is one observation. The working object is an AI Value Investigation Case—not a new product object. During the test it can be implemented through an existing AI Case, Problem record extension, or App Engine configuration. It keeps the decision and the learning in one place.

MovePlain meaning
1. Name the decisionState what could change, who owns it, the money at stake, the cost of waiting, and the realistic alternatives.
2. Keep explanations separateDo not turn the strongest correlation into “the cause.” Preserve plausible technical, workflow, control, behavior, measurement, and organizational explanations.
3. Compare the evidenceShow what supports and weakens each explanation, what is missing, and what cannot currently be observed. Compare successful, weak, and ambiguous cases when the contrast can separate explanations.
4. Put explanations in action statesEnough evidence to act; priority to test; park for now; contradicted; or currently unobservable.
5. Decide whether learning is worth itAsk whether the stakes and reducible uncertainty plausibly justify the cost and delay of learning. If yes, run the cheapest safe test likely to change the decision. Otherwise stop investigating.

Worked example. An AI service-desk assistant is used on most eligible incidents. Quality appears stable and overall resolution time is flat, but one business unit improves while another does not. Live explanations include harder case mix among AI users, approval delay consuming time saved, knowledge differences, and rework shifted downstream. Compare case mix, user experience, approval paths, knowledge use, reopens, and downstream work. If approval delay is consuming otherwise real gains, test the workflow—not the model. If the evidence remains weak and the next test costs more than the decision is worth, stop.

The idea is allowed to lose

A serious market test must give existing practice and competing approaches a fair chance to win. Record what each design partner would actually have done instead and compare against that—not against “no analysis.”

Commercial conflict. Some honest investigations should recommend no additional ServiceNow spend, a smaller AI deployment, another provider, or a non-AI answer. The market test should measure whether better-targeted expansion, trust, reusable learning, and long-run customer value outweigh the near-term revenue it deliberately gives up.

Run the market test with a hard resource ceiling

Do not invent a customer count and call it rigor. Set the maximum Impact/FDE time and customer-specific setup you are willing to spend before recruitment. Use a small number of paying design partners, no dedicated product engineering, and stop when the ceiling is reached. Only committed budget counts as demand.

Why you can test this without a platform bet

Your potential advantage is not superior causal inference. It is workflow proximity: the AI asset, workflow evidence, accountable people, approved change, and later operating result can often be connected in the same operating environment. That advantage weakens when decisive evidence is off-platform, mostly qualitative, or spread across organizations you cannot observe cheaply. Those cases count against the proposition.

RoleInitial hypothesis
Economic buyerCIO or VP of IT Operations accountable for service-desk economics and the return on AI-enabled ITSM.
Operating ownerService-desk or ITSM leader who can supply evidence, authorize workflow changes, and own the operating result.
Investigation partnersAI owner, analyst or process team, Impact/FDE, and control partners as needed.
Likely first budgetITSM optimization, Impact/customer success, or AI transformation—not a new “causal analytics” software budget.

Measure the delta before claiming one

Public materials establish many of the people, records, and improvement mechanisms already involved. They do not establish the end-to-end time, cost, reasoning quality, or economic outcome of post-deployment AI-management decisions. For every design partner, record the real substitute and measure both processes.

Evidence taskWhat to collect
Measure the baselineElapsed time, active expert hours, teams involved, missing-data wait, approval delay, test cost, time to measured outcome, and which substitute actually performed the work.
Inspect the reasoningWhich explanations were considered? What supported and weakened them? What was never observed? Which analyses reopened? Which decisions remained unsupported?
Measure the moneyWhat value was captured, protected, foregone, or lost while the decision remained unresolved? Count time savings only when they become capacity, quality, staffing, operating cost, revenue, or avoided risk.
Measure false certaintyHow often did the structured method produce a confident causal story that later evidence weakened or contradicted? Faster decisions do not count if unsupported certainty rises.
Claim only the differenceThe claim is not “our agent finds root causes.” It is that the structured investigation changes the action, shortens the time to a justified action, or shows that further investigation or action is not worth taking—better than the customer’s actual substitute.

Build only what paid evidence earns

Before the test begins, agree that the following statements must become true before you authorize expansion. If they do not, narrow, repackage, partner, or stop.

GateEvidence before expansion
The problem is real.Repeated high-value AI-management decisions remain unresolved after ordinary analysis, and the delay or error costs enough to matter.
Customers pay.Customers commit real budget for bounded investigations; interest without budget is not market evidence.
Decisions improve without more false certainty.The method changes the action, shortens the time to a justified action, or correctly stops further action versus the best realistic substitute—without increasing unsupported causal claims.
Evidence is affordable.Data, interviews, setup, and delay cost less than the plausible value of improving the decision.
The work repeats.Comparable cases reuse the same case structure, evidence requests, comparisons, action states, and tests.
Expert cost falls.Expert hours and correction burden fall while decision quality and neutrality hold.
Existing configuration or visibility is not enough.Paid cases reveal either a repeatable capability gap or a repeatable discoverability/packaging gap worth fixing. If current products already work and customers can find them, stop.
You capture value within a precommitted horizon.Paid delivery, successful expansion, justified attach, automation, reusable learning, or an intentional premium service outweigh foregone bad revenue and the cost of maintaining the motion.
Final judgment. Spend little to learn whether AI-enabled workflow outcomes can become reusable knowledge that materially improves customers’ next AI-management decision—and whether you should own any part of that loop. Let current products, better visibility, internal teams, outside advisers, acting immediately, doing nothing, and even lower ServiceNow spend beat the idea when they are better. If paid evidence still shows a repeatable customer need, improving decisions, falling delivery cost, and a gap you are well positioned to close, then decide what durable form captures the value. If not, stop—having learned cheaply rather than built expensively.

Use this to decide whether an AI-enabled workflow deserves deeper investigation, structure the explanation, choose the next evidence-gathering move, and retain what was learned.

2. Admit only cases worth learning about

  • Materially different actions remain plausible.
  • The decision can materially change cost, capacity, quality, risk, or revenue.
  • At least one important uncertainty can realistically be reduced.
  • There is a credible case that better information is worth its likely cost and delay.

If any answer is no: act with current evidence, use the existing process, route elsewhere, or stop investigating.
